Meet "The Hammer"

A Builder. A Father. A Distributor.

Braxton McAllister is a third-generation heavy-equipment distributor. His grandfather sold presses. His father sold bigger presses. Braxton looked at the largest press in the catalog and asked the question no career politician had the courage to ask: what if it were for something?

For 26 years he has run McAllister Industrial Supply out of the same cinderblock office off Route 51, meeting payroll, sponsoring Little League, and seeing no contradiction there.

"I didn't go to some fancy Ivy League school to learn how torque works. I learned it on the factory floor."

Braxton lives in the district with his wife, Deb, and their golden retriever, Gasket. Their three children are away at boarding school, out of state, at Deb's insistence.

Straight Answers to Tough Questions

Isn't a Child Crushing Machine bad for families?

Families tell us their top concerns are jobs, taxes, and noise. The Machine addresses all three. Our internal polling (n=14, all coalition board members) shows 100% of respondents hold a favorable or very favorable view. Braxton has always said the strongest family is a solvent one.

Why did you eliminate emergency stop buttons and perimeter railings?

Every emergency stop is, by definition, a stop. Stops are downtime, and downtime is deficit. An independent study commissioned and edited by the campaign found railings slow the line by up to 11%. Braxton trusts hardworking Americans to decide for themselves where to stand.

How will we pay for the machine?

With $480 million in municipal bonds, repaid from the surplus the Machine generates, as projected by the firm selling the bonds. Any shortfall will be covered by a modest convenience fee on library cards. Not one dime of new taxes on anyone who attends our donor retreats.

What is Braxton’s compromise for undecided voters?

Braxton hears you. That's why he's proposing the Tuesdays & Thursdays Pilot Program: an 18-month phased rollout in which the Machine operates only two days a week while a Blue-Ribbon Committee studies whether to add Wednesdays. On all other days the facility will be available for ribbon-cuttings and corporate retreats.
